Welcome to my academic website.

I am Maria Andrade, a Ph.D. student in Hydrology at the University of Arizona, with a minor in Remote Sensing. My work sits at the intersection of hydrologic modeling, remote sensing, groundwater-surface water interactions, flood hazards, and climate impacts.

My current research is supported by the U.S. Department of Energy and focuses on hydrological processes and groundwater-surface water interactions in the Colorado River corridor. I am especially interested in using modeling and field evidence to assess remediation effectiveness, habitat suitability, persistence, and connectivity under current and future climate conditions.

Before joining the University of Arizona, I completed an M.Sc. in Hydraulics and Sanitation at the University of Sao Paulo, where my thesis examined deterministic and probabilistic methods for assessing dam-break impacts through hydrodynamic modeling. I also hold a B.Sc. in Civil Engineering from the Federal University of Pernambuco, where my undergraduate research examined socioeconomic benefits of water-supply infrastructure in Pernambuco, Brazil.

Research
======

My research interests include:

- Remote sensing
- Surface water modeling
- Groundwater modeling
- Dam-break modeling
- Climate change modeling
- Hydrology and hydraulics
- Optimization and large-scale sensing systems
- Machine learning for water-resources applications

I am broadly motivated by questions that connect water, risk, infrastructure, and ecosystems: how hydrologic systems respond to disturbance, how uncertainty propagates through flood and habitat assessments, and how modeling can support better decisions in a changing climate.

Current focus
======

At the University of Arizona, I work on hydraulic and thermal controls on river-corridor habitat suitability and shallow groundwater-river recharge dynamics near the Moab UMTRA site. This work combines hydrologic process understanding with spatial analysis, remote sensing, and modeling tools.

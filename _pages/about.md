<section class="profile-hero">
  <p class="eyebrow">Hydrology · Hydraulic modeling · Remote sensing</p>
  <h1>Understanding how water, infrastructure, and ecosystems respond to disturbance.</h1>
  <p>
    I am a Ph.D. student in Hydrology at the University of Arizona, with a minor in Remote Sensing.
    My work connects field-scale hydrologic processes, groundwater–surface water interactions,
    flood hazards, and climate-sensitive river corridors.
  </p>
  <p>
    My current research focuses on the Colorado River corridor, where I study how hydrological,
    hydraulic, and thermal processes shape habitat suitability, persistence, and connectivity under
    present and future conditions. Before joining the University of Arizona, I completed an M.Sc. in
    Hydraulics and Sanitation at the University of São Paulo, where I worked on deterministic and
    probabilistic dam-break flood modeling using hydrodynamic simulations and uncertainty analysis.
  </p>
  <p>
    I also bring engineering practice into my research. From 2020 to 2024, I worked as a water
    resources engineer on large water infrastructure and sanitation projects in Northeast Brazil,
    including hydraulic modeling, transient analysis, pumping systems, reservoirs, and pressurized
    distribution networks for rural communities.
  </p>

<section class="section-block">
  <h2>Research storyline</h2>
  <div class="research-grid">
    <article class="research-card">
      <h3>River corridors and habitats</h3>
      <p>
        I study how hydrodynamic and thermal controls influence habitat suitability and connectivity
        in regulated river corridors, with applications to ecosystem protection and remediation.
      </p>
    </article>
    <article class="research-card">
      <h3>Flood hazards and dam-break uncertainty</h3>
      <p>
        My M.Sc. research developed deterministic and probabilistic dam-break flood maps using
        hydrodynamic models, Monte Carlo simulations, and uncertainty-aware impact assessment.
      </p>
    </article>
    <article class="research-card">
      <h3>Water infrastructure resilience</h3>
      <p>
        I have worked on hydraulic design and transient analysis for transmission mains, pumping
        stations, reservoirs, and rural water distribution systems in semi-arid regions.
      </p>
    </article>
    <article class="research-card">
      <h3>Green infrastructure and managed aquifer recharge</h3>
      <p>
        Earlier research connected managed aquifer recharge, digital green infrastructure, and
        water resources management for drought-prone regions in Brazil.
      </p>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

  <p>
    My current work combines hydrology, hydraulics, remote sensing, and modeling to understand how
    water systems behave when stressed by climate variability, infrastructure operations, and natural
    hazards. I am especially interested in models that are useful beyond a single case study: tools
